The clinical problem

Discontinuation is now counted as failure

Under WHO's revised outcome definitions, a patient who stops therapy — even temporarily, even if they later resume — is classified as a treatment failure. In this cohort, 479 of 1,086 patients (44%) fell into that category, most often after thrombocytopenia, liver dysfunction, or hyperuricemia driven by first-line drugs.

Because these adverse reactions are detectable in routine bloodwork before treatment begins, an admission-day risk score gives clinicians a window to intervene — through closer monitoring, dose adjustment, or enhanced patient education — before symptoms emerge.

Disclaimer

A research demonstration, not a diagnostic device

This site reproduces the methodology of a single-center retrospective study (Changzhou Third People's Hospital, n=1,086) with no external validation. Predictions here are for educational exploration only and must not inform real treatment decisions. See Methodology for the study's own stated limitations.
