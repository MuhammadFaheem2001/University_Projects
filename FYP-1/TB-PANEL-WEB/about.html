About this project

Turning a retrospective cohort into a working screening tool

This site operationalizes the findings of a 2025 retrospective study on tuberculosis treatment outcomes, translating its best-performing model into an interactive panel clinicians and researchers can explore.

The study

Wang et al., "Prediction of tuberculosis treatment outcomes using biochemical markers with machine learning," BMC Infectious Diseases (2025) 25:229.

Data were collected retrospectively from 2,520 TB patients admitted to Changzhou Third People's Hospital, China, between November 2019 and January 2023. After excluding HIV infection, pregnancy, and non-active TB, 1,990 patients remained; 904 were lost to follow-up, leaving 1,086 patients with a confirmed outcome — cured or treatment failure — for modelling.

The study's central contribution was expanding "treatment failure" to include discontinuation of therapy, in line with WHO's 2021 revised outcome definitions — not just mortality or drug resistance, which are comparatively rare and harder to model from routine data.

Why this matters

Most TB prognosis research targets rare, severe endpoints — death, multidrug resistance — using omics or imaging data that isn't available in most clinics. This study instead targeted the far more common outcome of treatment discontinuation, using only a routine admission blood panel.

That combination — a common outcome, an inexpensive input — is what makes the result practical to deploy: no genomic sequencing, no specialist imaging, just six values a hospital lab already produces on day one.

441 of the 479 treatment failures in this cohort were attributed specifically to drug-related adverse reactions leading to discontinuation, not resistance or death — underscoring that most failure here is a preventable, monitorable event.
