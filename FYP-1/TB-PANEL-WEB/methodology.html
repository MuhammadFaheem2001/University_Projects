Pipeline

From 94 raw indicators to a 6-feature model

A transparent walk-through of preprocessing, feature selection, model training, and validation — plus the study's own stated limitations.

Pipeline steps

  1. Cohort assembly. 2,520 TB patients screened; HIV, pregnancy, and non-active TB excluded, leaving 1,990. Of those, 904 were lost to follow-up and dropped from modelling, leaving 1,086 patients with a confirmed cured/failure outcome.
  2. Feature set. 94 admission-day clinical indicators — demographics, medical history, and routine biochemical results.
  3. Train/test split. 80% training, 20% held-out test, stratified so the test set stays representative of the outcome balance.
  4. Feature selection (7 methods). Mutual Information, F-Test, Spearman, Kendall's tau, Maximal Information Coefficient, T-Test, and Wilcoxon — each independently run on the training set only.
  5. Model training (12 classifiers). AdaBoost, Bagging, Decision Tree, ExtraTrees, Gaussian Naive Bayes, Gradient Boosting, KNN, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SGD Classifier, SVM, XGBoost.
  6. Cross-validation. 4-fold CV on the training set for each of the 84 selection × model combinations, then a single independent evaluation on the held-out test set.
  7. Explainability. SHAP applied to the top-performing models to rank feature contributions.

Why Mutual Information

MI quantifies statistical dependency between each candidate feature and the outcome label without assuming a linear relationship — well suited to biochemical markers whose relationship to treatment failure may be threshold-based rather than linear. It also reduced the feature set from 94 to 6 without materially hurting predictive performance, which matters for a tool meant to run on a routine admission panel.

Evaluation metrics

  • AUC — overall discrimination between cured and failure across all thresholds.
  • Accuracy — proportion of correct predictions on the test set.
  • Sensitivity — proportion of actual failures correctly flagged.
  • Specificity — proportion of actual cures correctly cleared.

The winning KNN + MI model reached AUC 0.87, accuracy 0.82, sensitivity 0.69, specificity 0.92 on the test set — strong at ruling cured patients in, weaker at catching every failure case, a known trade-off under class imbalance.

Stated limitations (from the source study)

High non-adherence / loss to follow-up

904 of 1,990 eligible patients (≈45%) were lost to follow-up and excluded from modelling, roughly halving the usable sample. Patients lost to follow-up may differ systematically from those retained, introducing potential bias and reducing statistical power.

No external validation

The study was conducted at a single center (Changzhou Third People's Hospital) without validation at other sites. Different hospitals' diagnostic and treatment protocols could shift both the discontinuation criteria and the model's applicability elsewhere.

Wiring this site to a real trained model

The backend (backend/server.py) currently uses a transparent, weighted scoring approximation of the KNN + MI model, calibrated from the SHAP values reported in the paper — useful for demoing the interface, not for clinical inference. To plug in an actual trained model:

  1. Train and export your model: joblib.dump(model, "backend/artifacts/model.pkl")
  2. Export your fitted scaler the same way, if one was used in training.
  3. In server.py, uncomment the MODEL_LOAD block and replace the body of heuristic_predict() with a call to model.predict_proba(...).
  4. Keep the response shape (prediction, probability_failure, contributions, etc.) so the frontend needs no changes.
