Why the model says what it says

A model that flags risk without explaining itself is hard for a clinician to trust. SHAP values quantify exactly how much each biomarker pushed a prediction up or down from the cohort's baseline failure rate of 47.1%.

Global feature importance

Average SHAP contribution across the 218-patient test set. 5′-NT and uric acid dominate; the remaining four features contribute smaller, still-positive amounts.

Reading a waterfall chart

Each prediction starts at the baseline probability — 0.471, the average failure rate across the test set — then every biomarker nudges the estimate up or down until it lands on the final predicted probability.

  • 5′-NT (+0.16): the single largest driver. Elevated levels signal liver stress before treatment even begins.
  • Uric acid (+0.16): equally influential; pyrazinamide is known to raise uric acid further during treatment.
  • Globulin (+0.12): reflects immune activity; markedly elevated levels can indicate immune-mediated tissue damage risk.
  • Cystatin C, creatinine, AST (+0.03 each): smaller but consistently positive — supporting signals of kidney and liver strain.

Reproduction of the SHAP waterfall reported for the test-set baseline case (Fig. 3).

Liver

5′-NT, Globulin, AST

These three markers are closely tied to liver function. Anti-TB drugs — particularly isoniazid and rifampicin — are well-documented causes of drug-induced liver injury via oxidative stress and hepatocyte ferroptosis.

Kidney

Uric acid, Creatinine, Cystatin C

These reflect renal clearance. Pyrazinamide and ethambutol inhibit renal excretion of uric acid, and baseline elevation predicts a higher risk of treatment-limiting hyperuricemia.

Interpretation, not causation

SHAP explains what the model learned from correlational patterns in this cohort — it is not proof of a causal mechanism, and should be read alongside clinical judgment.
